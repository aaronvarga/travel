Family Trip · Jun 10-23, 2027

Turkish Riviera
Antalya to Fethiye

Twelve warm-water nights on Turkey's Turquoise Coast: Antalya's old harbor and Roman ruins, Kas/Kalkan's clear coves and Kekova boat day, then Fethiye/Oludeniz for Blue Lagoon, Butterfly Valley, Saklikent, and an easy Dalaman exit. The route stays coastal, skips Istanbul/Cappadocia, and uses one rental car so the trip feels like a Riviera vacation rather than a country sampler.

The Plan at a Glance

Three bases on the Turquoise Coast: Antalya, Kas/Kalkan, and Fethiye/Oludeniz

This is the family-balanced Turkish Riviera route: no domestic flight, no ferry dependency, and no long inland detour. Every stop below has a real cost band, weather/water note, plain-food restaurant options, and an alternate if heat, waves, or kid energy changes the day.

Route

PIT -> Antalya / AYT -> Kas or Kalkan -> Fethiye or Oludeniz -> Dalaman / DLM -> PIT

Open-jaw flights avoid a six-hour backtrack to Antalya at the end.

Lodging split

3 + 4 + 5 nights

Short arrival base in Antalya, four nights for the best water around Kas, then five nights in the Fethiye/Oludeniz area for the strongest beach-and-boat finish.

Rough all-in

~$10.2k-$15.9k · family of 4

Flights are the swing item; Turkish lodging and food are helpful, but AYT/DLM open-jaw fares decide whether this stays under the $15k cap.

Trip feel

~45% water · 30% towns · 25% nature/history

Warm-water days lead the plan, with ruins, old towns, canyon walking, and short hikes keeping it from becoming an all-beach trip.

Plan Health-Check

What to lock, watch, and expect

Research updated July 2026 for a June 2027 family trip, with kids age 13 and 8 in the trip year. 2027 flights and some attraction prices must still be re-checked when bookable.

Strong fit

Best remaining warm-water Europe-style answer

June sea temperatures are already comfortable on this coast: Antalya is commonly around 25-26 C / 77-79 F, and Kas is around 24 C / 75 F. That is a materially warmer water profile than Portugal, California, or early-June Atlantic options.

Flight gate

The airfare quote decides whether this is a finalist

Recent public fare signals put PIT-AYT around $900-$1,300 pp round-trip, while PIT-DLM/DLM-PIT can price higher. Treat $5.6k family airfare as the cap-fit gate; above that, the high end drifts over $15k.

PTO win

Juneteenth saves a vacation day

Depart after work Thu Jun 10, arrive Antalya Fri Jun 11, use observed Juneteenth on Fri Jun 18 as the Fethiye transfer day, and fly home Wed Jun 23. That makes a 12-night trip about 8 PTO days.

Heat

Ruins need morning starts

Antalya, Perge, Aspendos, Patara, and Kayakoy can be punishing by midday in June. Put ruins before lunch, make boat/water days the afternoon default, and keep one Fethiye day deliberately loose.

Driving

The D400 is scenic but not passive

The coastal road between Antalya, Kas, and Fethiye is gorgeous and mostly straightforward, but it has mountain curves, slow trucks, and cliffside stretches. Use daylight-only transfers and do not schedule hard dinner reservations after long drives.

Food fit

Picky eaters are easier here than the map implies

Every base has pizza, pasta, burgers, fries, pide, kofte, grilled chicken, and hotel-pool fallback meals. Turkish breakfasts, bakeries, and pide are a real advantage for the 8-year-old.

Best Time to Go

Jun 10-23 is the cleanest early-summer version

The Turkish Riviera gets better for swimming as June progresses, but July and August bring sharper heat, higher resort pricing, and heavier beach crowds. This window uses observed Juneteenth without pushing deep into peak season.

Length verdict

Where We Are Staying

Three home bases, one westbound rental car

Each base has a distinct job: arrival and ruins, best coves and Kekova, then the broadest beach/boat/canyon finish.

3 nights · Jun 11-13

Antalya · Kaleici or Konyaalti

Arrival base + Roman ruins

Kaleici is best for atmosphere and no-car evenings; Konyaalti is easier if the priority is beach, parking, and apartment space. Antalya earns the first base because it is the natural AYT arrival point and gives you Perge/Aspendos without a separate inland move.

Book: Choose either a Kaleici family suite with parking arranged in writing, or a Konyaalti apartment/hotel with pool and easy garage parking. Plan roughly $120-$240/night for family-capable midrange lodging.

4 nights · Jun 14-17

Kas or Kalkan

Coves, Kaputas, Patara, Kekova

Kas is the better town base if you want harbor evenings and boat trips; Kalkan is more villa/pool-forward and closer to Kaputas. Four nights prevents the best water stretch from becoming a rushed drive-through.

Book: Prioritize parking, AC, a pool, and either walking access to Kas town or a short drive/taxi plan in Kalkan. Budget about $160-$320/night in June for a family apartment, small hotel suite, or modest villa.

5 nights · Jun 18-22

Fethiye, Hisaronu/Ovacik, or Oludeniz

Blue Lagoon + boats + canyon

Fethiye town is practical for boats and food; Ovacik/Hisaronu often gives better family space and pools; Oludeniz is closest to the lagoon but busier. Five nights makes room for water, canyon, ghost village, and a flex day before flying home from Dalaman.

Book: For this family, favor a pool apartment or aparthotel in Ovacik/Hisaronu with easy taxi/dolmus access to Oludeniz and Fethiye harbor. Plan roughly $130-$280/night.

  • Book apartments/aparthotels with pools. Turkey's lodging value is best when you avoid full-service resorts but still get AC, space, kitchen, and pool.
  • Make Kekova shared unless it is the signature splurge. Private boats are wonderful but can add $500-$800 over shared seats.
  • Skip daily beach clubs. Use public beaches, hotel pool resets, and paid loungers only on true all-day water days.
  • Self-drive ruins. Guided Perge/Aspendos/Termessos days multiply costs quickly for four people.
  • Use pide/kofte/pizza nights. Casual dinners are one of the best Turkey budget levers and still work for picky eaters.

Worth the splurge

  • Private Kekova boat. Best single comfort splurge if the family wants shade, quieter pacing, and swim-stop control.
  • Pool-forward Kas/Kalkan lodging. Worth paying for because the middle base carries the best water stretch.
  • Small Oludeniz boat. Choose a no-loud-music or smaller route if Butterfly Valley scenery matters.
  • One guided ruins morning. Use a guide only where story matters most, probably Perge/Aspendos, not every site.
  • Paragliding. Iconic in Oludeniz, but optional and age/nerve/operator-dependent.

Documents

US family basics

  • No Turkey e-Visa for ordinary US passports. Turkey's MFA lists ordinary US passport holders as visa-exempt up to 90 days in any 180-day period.
  • Passports still matter. Use the usual six-month-validity comfort rule even when formal visa friction is low.
  • Carry license + IDP. Turkey car rentals may ask for an International Driving Permit alongside the US license.

Flights

Avoid routing traps

  • Dalaman exit is the point. Do not backtrack to Antalya unless airfare savings clearly beat the time cost.
  • Istanbul connection. If Turkish Airlines prices well, IST gives easy onward hops to Antalya and Dalaman; still leave real connection padding.
  • Seat assignments. Pay early to keep the four seats together on long-haul legs.

Driving

Turquoise Coast rhythm

  • Daylight only. The D400 is beautiful but curvy; transfer days should finish before dark.
  • Parking in old towns. Kaleici and Kas are not where you improvise parking with luggage. Confirm details before booking.
  • Fuel and snacks. Top up before long coast drives and carry water; the prettiest stretches are not the easiest service stops.

Water Days

Make the heat work for you

  • Beach mornings. Kaputas, Blue Lagoon, and Patara are better before peak parking and sun.
  • Rash guards. Useful on boat days where shade is limited and sunscreen reapplication gets old.
  • Saklikent shoes. Bring old sneakers or rent grip shoes; flip-flops are a bad idea in the gorge.

Food

Picky-eater plan

  • Pide is your friend. It reads like Turkish pizza and is widely available.
  • Use big-menu restaurants strategically. Bella Gusto, BigChefs-style places, and Kas Italian spots are there for tired nights.
  • Breakfasts are easy. Turkish hotel breakfasts, bakeries, simit, fruit, yogurt, and eggs reduce morning friction.
