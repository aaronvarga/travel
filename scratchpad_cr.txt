 The Plan at a Glance 
 Volcano and cloud forest up front, a long beach finale 
 Unlike the Portugal and Greece plans' even split across four bases, this trip front-loads two short, activity-dense stops and then settles into one long Pacific-coast base — seven straight nights so the second half of the trip isn't spent packing and driving. Every stop below has a map pin, price, a picky-kid restaurant pick, an alternative, and travel-blog links. 
 Route SJO → La Fortuna/Arenal → Monteverde → Manuel Antonio → SJO One clean clockwise loop by rental 4x4 — no backtracking, and the last drive before the flight home is the shortest one.  Plan Health-Check 
 What to lock, watch, and expect 
 A critical review of the plan against your family & June constraints — researched July 2026. Kids assumed 13 & 8 in June 2027. 
 Act now 
 Manuel Antonio National Park requires an advance reservation — no walk-ups 
 Tickets are sold only through the SINAC online portal , not at the gate, and the park is closed Tuesdays . Even in green season this is the one hard, bookable constraint in the whole itinerary — reserve the slot the moment dates are locked, and build Day 7 around whatever entry window is actually available. 
 Heads-up 
 This is genuinely wet season — and Monteverde's access roads are unpaved 
 Expect a real afternoon downpour most days, not just a chance of one. Both routes into Monteverde involve gravel/dirt stretches, and standard rental insurance excludes unpaved-road and river-crossing damage regardless of coverage tier — a 4x4 SUV is non-negotiable, and it's exactly why Osa Peninsula didn't make this itinerary at all. 
 Heads-up 
 Quetzal sightings in June are a coin flip, not a guarantee 
 Peak quetzal season is February–April; by June the birds are moving toward the Pacific lowlands. Even with a guide, June sighting odds run roughly 20–40% , not the 80%+ of peak season. Set expectations with the 8-year-old before the cloud forest walk — frame it as a rainforest hike that might get lucky. 
 Heads-up 
 La Fortuna Waterfall is 500+ steps down — and the climb back up is the real test 
 The descent takes ~10 minutes with handrails and benches; the return climb runs 20–30 minutes and is genuinely tiring for an 8-year-old in June heat. Don't schedule anything else strenuous the same day — the hanging bridges walk that follows it is deliberately flat and shaded. 
 Solid — keep it 
 PTO math checks out — identical efficiency to Portugal & Greece 
 Depart Thursday evening (Jun 10), not Friday. You work the full day Thursday — no PTO for an evening flight — and land in San José Friday afternoon with the whole rest of the day still ahead. No holiday assist needed this time (Juneteenth 2027 falls on a Saturday) — the Thu-depart/Wed-return geometry alone delivers 8 PTO days for 12 nights away — 1.50 nights per PTO day , matching both prior trips exactly. 
 Now fixed 
 Picky-eater food — covered at every stop 
 Every stop below has 2–3 family-vetted restaurants with plain pizza/pasta/burgers/fries/casados, plus real prices where they're findable. La Fortuna's sodas, Santa Elena's casual spots, and Quepos/Manuel Antonio's beach-town restaurants all have an easy kid option.

 Why we didn't chase the July “veranillo” dry spell 
 Costa Rica's mid-summer dry pocket (locally the veranillo , or “little summer”) is real, but it's concentrated in Guanacaste — the driest coastal region, which isn't part of this route. La Fortuna and Manuel Antonio run essentially flat rainfall June through August, and Monteverde's cloud forest is misty and wet regardless of which week you pick . Shifting the whole trip a month later to catch a dry spell that mostly benefits a region we're not visiting isn't a real trade. 
 Why the base plan wins outright 
 Flight fares are essentially flat across the entire Jun 8–Aug 20 window — the real fare difference is winter dry-season vs. summer green-season, not week-to-week within summer. Weather for our three regions doesn't meaningfully improve later in the window either, and early August starts trending toward the September–October rain peak, most noticeably on the central Pacific coast where Manuel Antonio sits. The base plan also uniquely lands exactly on the Jun 23–26 blackout's first day, needs no calendar renegotiation, and keeps the same proven Thu-depart/Wed-return PTO trick at 1.50 nights per PTO day. 
 Pros of the base plan 
 Same 1.50 nights/PTO ratio as both the Portugal and Greece plans — no extra vacation-day cost for a third international trip in three years. Lands home exactly as the Pittsburgh commitment begins — zero buffer wasted, zero risk of the two overlapping. No meaningful weather or fare upside anywhere else in the 10-week window to justify moving it. 
 Cons / open risks 
 Early June sits inside La Fortuna and Manuel Antonio's wetter pocket of the summer — though the difference from July/August is modest for these two regions specifically. Zero float before the Pittsburgh commitment — any travel delay on Jun 23 eats directly into it. Manuel Antonio National Park reservations need to be locked as soon as the trip is booked, regardless of which week — this doesn't get easier by waiting. 
 Overall verdict 
 Keep the base plan: depart Thu Jun 10 evening, return Wed Jun 23. We scanned the full 10-week window and found no region-specific weather or fare pocket good enough to justify the schedule risk of moving closer to — or past — the Jun 23–26 Pittsburgh blackout. Book Manuel Antonio National Park and the rental car first; everything else in this stretch has real availability, not just a price advantage, to protect.

 Where We Are Staying 
 Three home bases 
 Each base is chosen for AC, a washer, and — where the destination allows — a pool or short beach walk. 
 3 nights · Jun 11–13 
 La Fortuna, near Arenal Volcano & hot-springs base 
 Easy parking, a short drive to the volcano trails, hot springs, and the waterfall — the whole La Fortuna stretch is contained within a 15-minute radius of town. 
 Book: Hotel or aparthotel with AC, a pool, and (optionally) a volcano-view room — view rooms run $50–100/night more than a standard room, and the view is genuinely worth it on a clear evening. 
 2 nights · Jun 14–15 
 Santa Elena / Monteverde Cloud forest base 
 Walkable town center, close to both the Santa Elena Reserve and the 100% Aventura canopy tour — no long drives needed for either day here. 
 Book: Cabina or small lodge with AC (or at least good ventilation — Monteverde is cool enough that AC matters less than at the other two bases) and hot water; confirm the access road condition with your hotel directly before driving in. 
 7 nights · Jun 16–22 
 Manuel Antonio / Quepos Beach & rainforest base 
 The longest single stay of the trip — close enough to walk to Playa Espadilla, a short drive to the national park, Quepos restaurants, and the marina for the catamaran. 
 Book: A mid-range hotel/condo with AC and a pool (~$180–300/night) is the sweet spot for seven nights; a private villa with its own pool (Tulemar-style, $600+/night) is the splurge if the budget allows for one base to feel like a real vacation rental. 
 Getting Around 
 One rental car, one loop, no drop fee 
 A single 4x4 SUV rental covers the whole 12 nights — the route is a clean clockwise loop back to SJO, so there's no one-way drop charge. Price bands are 2025–2026 data — 2027 rates aren't bookable yet. 
 Rental Car · 4x4 SUV, ~12 days 
 Vehicle Compact/intermediate 4x4 (RAV4-class) Daily rate ~$70–112/day incl. mandatory insurance Est. 12-day total ~$1,000–1,700 Green-season note 10–35% below Dec–Apr peak rates 
 Costa Rican law requires a basic third-party liability policy (SLI) on every rental — it's charged directly by the company and can't be waived by a credit card or US auto policy. That covers liability only; add CDW on top for collision coverage. 
 Insurance & Road Rules 
 Mandatory SLI ~$10–25/day, non-waivable Optional CDW/full ~$13–25+/day more Excluded regardless of tier River crossings, unauthorized unpaved roads License US license valid 90 days; IDP recommended, not required 
 River crossings and off-authorized-road driving void coverage under every policy tier, no exceptions — this is exactly why the Monteverde legs and any Osa-style routing stay off the self-drive plan entirely. 
 Fuel & Tolls 
 Gasoline ~$1.65–1.70/liter (regular) Total transfer distance ~600–650 km + local driving Est. fuel, whole trip ~$130–180 Tolls (Route 27, SJO area) ~$8–9 one-way, cash or card 
 No tolls exist past the SJO area once you're on Route 34 toward Manuel Antonio — the rest of the loop is toll-free.

 Travel Tips 
 The stuff nobody tells you 
 Non-obvious logistics that make driving, hiking, and booking smoother — researched July 2026. Anything tied to 2027 rules or prices is flagged to re-check ~3 months out. 
 Book in roughly this order — exact target dates (trip departs Thu Jun 10, 2027) 
 Flights + 4x4 rental car · as soon as 2027 fares load Manuel Antonio National Park reservation · the moment dates are locked, via the SINAC portal Savegre River rafting & Damas Island mangrove tour · 4–6 weeks out Baldi Hot Springs / EcoTermales session · 1–2 weeks out Sunset catamaran & Nauyaca shuttle · 1–2 weeks out Restaurant reservations (splurge dinners only) · a few days ahead in June 
 Before you go — documents Months ahead — don't leave to the airport US citizens need no visa — a 90-day entry stamp on arrival is standard. Airlines commonly enforce 6-months passport validity beyond arrival , stricter than Costa Rica's own rule — plan around the stricter standard for all four passports. Proof of onward/return travel is typically required at check-in or entry — keep a printed return itinerary handy. Check passports now. Kids' passports are only valid 5 years — the most common trip-killer. Rental car & insurance Booking + day-of The mandatory SLI liability policy can't be waived by any credit card or US auto policy — budget for it as a fixed cost, not an optional add-on. River crossings void coverage under every policy tier. Never drive through standing water on any route, even ones that look shallow. Confirm your specific route (especially into Monteverde) is an "authorized road" with the rental company before you drive it — ask explicitly, don't assume. A US license is valid for 90 days of tourist driving — an International Driving Permit isn't legally required but is a useful Spanish-language backup. Driving days — go easy Three transfer days, decreasing in length SJO → La Fortuna (~3 hrs), La Fortuna → Monteverde (~3.5 hrs), Monteverde → Manuel Antonio (~4 hrs). Each one is fully paved except Monteverde's own access road. Avoid driving after dark — unlit, narrow, curvy roads with no shoulders or guardrails are the norm outside main highways. Fill the tank before any of the three transfer days — gas stations thin out well before Monteverde and around Dominical. Weather & wet-season driving June–August is genuine green season Expect a real afternoon downpour most days , not just a chance of one — schedule outdoor activities for morning departures across the whole trip. Monteverde stays misty and wet regardless of the exact week — pack real layers, not just a rain jacket. Potholes and occasional livestock on rural roads are normal — drive a little slower than GPS travel-time estimates assume. Timed tickets & heat management Book ahead — one sells out, several bake you alive by midday Manuel Antonio NP reservations are SINAC-portal-only and the park is closed Tuesdays. This is the trip's one truly mandatory advance booking. Do the La Fortuna Waterfall and Arenal Volcano NP hikes by 8–9am — both trails get hot and crowded, and the reliable afternoon rain makes early starts a real strategy, not just a preference. Damas Island's kayak departure is tide-dependent — the operator sets the exact time once booked; hold the rest of that day loose. Smart packing Free, but decisive Pack real rain layers, not just a poncho. Green season means a genuine afternoon downpour most days across all three regions. Get an eSIM (Airalo) before you land. Coverage drops in the Monteverde hills — download offline maps as backup. A light layer for Monteverde evenings — the cloud forest elevation runs 8–10°F cooler than La Fortuna or the coast. Reef-safe sunscreen and water shoes for the mangrove kayak, rafting, and beach days — volcanic-sand and river-rock footing both benefit from them.
