The Plan at a Glance

Best-balanced, Adriatic-swim, three bases

Same planning philosophy as the Portugal itinerary: water slightly ahead of hiking, enough old towns, and only three unpacking points. Croatia fixes the Atlantic-water problem with a warmer Adriatic route.

Route

PIT → Split → Hvar → Korčula → PIT

Four nights Split, three nights Hvar, five nights Korčula, moving south by ferry and flying home from Dubrovnik (DBV) via the Pelješac Bridge — without a Dubrovnik stay.

Lodging split

4 + 3 + 5 nights

Enough time for day trips without turning the trip into luggage management.

Rough all-in

~$8.6k–12.8k · family of 4

Flights are the big swing: PIT→Split / Dubrovnik→PIT should be budgeted as a planning band, not locked. Lodging now targets standard AC/washer apartments (no pool), Korčula runs noticeably cheaper than Dubrovnik did, and the Ston/Pelješac day is priced in rather than treated as an optional splurge.

Trip feel

~45% water · 30% towns · 25% nature

Beaches, boat days, old towns, oyster farms, salt lakes, and short hikes without overstuffing.

Plan Health-Check

What to lock, watch, and expect

A critical review of the Croatia plan against the corrected Jun 10–23 travel dates — refreshed July 2026. Kids assumed 13 & 8 in June 2027.

Act now

Flights are the fragile joint

PIT→Split has no direct or near-direct routing. Current public fare checks show no direct flights and roughly ~14-hour fastest routings; your recent fare band of $898–1,053 pp is realistic. Let the open-jaw air ticket set exact dates before locking nonrefundable villas.

Heads-up

Krka is not a waterfall-swim free-for-all

Skradinski buk swimming remains off-limits. Krka permits swimming only in marked zones at Roški slap, Stinice and Pisak from Jun 1–Sep 30, and conditions can still close them. Treat Krka as a boardwalk/waterfall day first.

Fixed

Dubrovnik is gone — and the Pelješac Bridge makes that easy

Korčula replaces Dubrovnik as the final base. The 2022 Pelješac Bridge now connects Korčula/Pelješac straight to the mainland, so the Jun 23 exit to Dubrovnik Airport (DBV) is a domestic ~2.5-hour ferry-plus-drive with zero Bosnia border crossings — the old Neum-corridor delay risk is gone entirely, even without visiting Dubrovnik itself.

Heads-up

Hvar ferries are easy — exact times are not

Split↔Hvar is frequent and short; Hvar↔Korčula runs Apr–Oct on fast catamarans (Krilo, TP Line, some Jadrolinija sailings) and is usually 50 min–1.5 hr / roughly €20–30 pp — a big improvement over the old 3h20m+ Hvar–Dubrovnik leg. Book once 2027 schedules post; skip a mainland car on Hvar or Korčula.

Solid — keep it

The water problem is actually fixed

June Adriatic sea temperatures around Split/Hvar/Korčula run roughly 70–72°F on this earlier mid-June window, warming as the trip moves south. That is a major improvement over the Portugal Atlantic plan’s 63–69°F kid-swim problem.

Now fixed

Picky-eater food is planned into every stop

Every stop below includes pizza/pasta/burger/fries fallbacks with prices and ratings. Croatia is easier than it looks for picky kids as long as you keep plain Italian-style options near each day’s endpoint.

Important blackout interpretation

The corrected Jun 10–23 plan uses 8 PTO days because the first date is an after-work departure and Friday Jun 18 is the observed Juneteenth holiday. It returns on Wednesday, June 23. If “must be home in Pittsburgh June 23–26” means home by the end of June 23, this works. If it means home and unavailable to travel all day June 23, the safer version is Jun 9–22 with the same route and same 8 PTO days.

What changed from the old Jun 11–24 draft

The trip is one day earlier overall, so Split starts on Friday Jun 11, Hvar starts Tuesday Jun 15, Korčula starts Friday Jun 18, and the final full day is Tuesday Jun 22. Costs are slightly friendlier than late June/July, but the water is also about a degree cooler at the front of the trip.

Dubrovnik removed, Korčula added

Aaron has already been to Dubrovnik, so the final 5-night base is now Korčula Town, with day trips to Pelješac (Ston walls, oysters, wine country) and Mljet National Park (salt-lake swimming). The route still flies home from Dubrovnik Airport (DBV) — that part hasn't changed — but the family never overnights in Dubrovnik itself. See the Where We Stay and Day by Day sections below.

Best overall answer

  • Jun 28–Jul 11 is the strongest all-factor window: 8 PTO, no blackout conflict, warmer water, better lavender, and still before the worst late-July/August pressure.
  • Jun 9–22 is the cleanest June answer if blackout protection matters literally.
  • Jun 10–23 is the best version of the corrected plan if arriving home on Jun 23 satisfies the obligation.

Why not peak July/August?

  • The PTO cap allows some later windows, but the experience gets worse: hotter old towns, more expensive lodging, and more ferry/boat pressure.
  • Korčula Old Town heat, cruise-ship day-trippers, restaurant reservations and beach shade costs all get less forgiving.
  • August adds Croatian holiday congestion without adding a US PTO advantage.

Recommendation

Absolute best: Jun 28–Jul 11. It keeps PTO to 8 days, clears the Jun 23–26 blackout, improves swim water and lavender odds, and avoids the worst late-July/August travel pressure. Keep Jun 10–23 only if June 23 can be a travel-home day and lower June costs matter more than warmer water.

Where We Are Staying

Three home bases

Each base is chosen for easy luggage, AC, washer and walkable beach access. Standard apartments beat pool villas here — sea swims cover the pool craving and keep the lodging line inside budget.

4 nights · Jun 11–14

Split

Arrival + mainland base

Best for Diocletian’s Palace, Trogir, Klis and Krka without adding a one-night stop.

Book: apartment/aparthotel near Riva/Bačvice/Varoš with AC, washer and quiet bedrooms; parking only needed for two rental days.

3 nights · Jun 15–17

Hvar Town

Island + beach base

Enough time for Pakleni Islands, beach coves, fortress sunset and a lavender-season interior loop.

Book: walkable lodging with AC and washer, or arranged port pickup if uphill. Pool is nice but not mandatory with real sea swims.

5 nights · Jun 18–22

Korčula Town

Final base

A smaller, cheaper walled Old Town than Dubrovnik, with Lumbarda's sandy beach, a Pelješac oyster-and-wine day, and Mljet's salt lakes all reachable without a car — then a bridge-connected, no-Bosnia-border exit to DBV.

Book: Old Town-adjacent apartment with AC, washer and no more than a short roll to the ferry quay. Korčula runs noticeably cheaper than Dubrovnik for a comparable family apartment.

Before you go — documents

Months ahead · Schengen rules

  • ETIAS is expected to be operating by June 2027. Official EU guidance says ETIAS starts in Q4 2026 with transitional/grace periods. Use only the official portal once live.
  • EES biometric borders are fully operational as of April 10, 2026. Expect first-entry face/fingerprint registration; under-12s do not give fingerprints.
  • Check passports now. Croatia is Schengen: passports need validity beyond the trip and kids’ passports expire faster.
  • Bring proof of parent/child relationship and notarized consent if one parent is not traveling.

Flights & airports

Booking + day-of

  • No direct PIT→Split. Prioritize one-ticket routings with enough gateway buffer over chasing a tiny fare savings.
  • Pay for seats together on transatlantic legs; do not rely on airport assignment with kids.
  • Use DBV for the return so you do not waste the last day crossing back to Split.
  • Keep transfer days light. Catamarans and summer winds can shift exact timing.

Arrival day — go private

Skip friction on no sleep

  • Pre-book SPU→Split transfer if arriving after a long connection; the bus is cheaper but less forgiving.
  • Do not pick up the rental on arrival. Split old town and jet lag are a bad combo.
  • Choose lodging for luggage access. Old-town charm with stairs is not charming with four bags.

Rental cars & ferries

Keep wheels limited

  • Rent only in Split for Krka/Trogir/Klis. Return before Hvar.
  • Child restraints matter. For an 8-year-old, bring a packable booster if height/fit is borderline; rental seats are inconsistent.
  • Ask rental agencies about ferry permission only if you change plans and take a car to an island.
  • Korčula is not a car base either. Old Town is pedestrianized; use the private driver/tour for the Pelješac day instead of self-driving across the bridge.

Parks & timed tickets

Rules changed in ways families notice

  • Krka: swimming only in marked zones at Roški slap, Stinice and Pisak, Jun 1–Sep 30; not below Skradinski buk.
  • Plitvice: no swimming. It is a world-class boardwalk/hiking day, not the answer to the kid-swimming brief.
  • Mljet: pay the 2026 high-season rate. €25 adult/€8 child (7–18), a return boat to Sveta Marija islet is included.
  • Book the Mljet boat and Pelješac driver 2–4 weeks ahead but keep weather flexibility.

Smart packing

Small things that matter

  • Pack water shoes for rocky Adriatic beaches, Lokrum and Hvar coves.
  • Bring reef-safe sunscreen, hats and lightweight coverups. Shade can be expensive.
  • Carry some cash for beach bars, taxis, lockers and small island vendors.
  • Download offline maps and ferry PDFs before crossing islands.
