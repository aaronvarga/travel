Mon - 6/7/2027

Arrive Athens, Plaka evening

Soft landing under the Acropolis

Est. $150 · taxi, easy dinner

SleepAthens (Plaka/Syntagma) · night 1 of 2
TransferATH airport -> Plaka ~40 min by taxi/metro
PlanCheck in, wander Plaka & Anafiotika, early dinner

Land in Athens, drop bags near Plaka, and let the first evening be gentle: the pedestrian lanes of Plaka and the whitewashed island-in-the-city pocket of Anafiotika are the perfect jet-lag stroll before the islands begin.

Travel day - arrive ATH, settle in Plaka.

Plaka & Anafiotika

PhotosIG · plakaIG · anafiotikaIG · athensFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Free to wander. Plaka is the old pedestrian quarter under the Acropolis — neoclassical lanes, tavernas, and souvenir stalls — and tucked into its upper slope is Anafiotika, a pocket of whitewashed, blue-doored Cycladic houses built by island masons in the 1840s. It is the trip’s gentle first taste of the islands, and a perfect jet-lag evening stroll.

Old town

Shaded, walkable, warm-evening. The narrow lanes hold shade even midday, and June evenings stay pleasant into the night. Anafiotika is residential — steep little stair-paths, worth the quiet respect (people live there).

Save Make Plaka your free arrival-evening walk: climb the Anafiotika stairs for the rooftop-to-Lycabettus view at golden hour and eat where the locals do, off the main tourist strip.
Splurge A sit-down dinner on a vine-shaded Plaka terrace, plus a loukoumades (honey-doughnut) stop the kids will remember longer than any ruin.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Plaka tavernas off the main lane — gyros, souvlaki, chips, and bread; the easiest picky-kid dinner in Athens
  • A Monastiraki pita counter — €3–4 pork/chicken gyros for a fast, cheap arrival-night meal
  • Loukoumades shop — hot honey doughnuts for dessert as the jet-lag treat
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Anafiotika at golden hour for the whitewashed-village preview and the best rooftop light.
  • National Garden for a shaded, free morning with the 8-year-old before the Acropolis.
  • Panathenaic (Kallimarmaro) Stadium for a quick run around the marble track.

Tue - 6/8/2027

The Acropolis

The Parthenon, then the Acropolis Museum

Est. $180 · site + museum tickets, dinner

SleepAthens (Plaka/Syntagma) · night 2 of 2
AnchorAcropolis (timed slot) + Acropolis Museum
Heat ruleGo at 8am open or after 6pm - midday is brutal

The one big culture day: the Acropolis at opening or late afternoon to beat the June heat, with the air-conditioned Acropolis Museum as the kid-friendly companion. Book the timed-entry slot in advance. Repack tonight for tomorrow’s ferry.

The Acropolis & the marble city

PhotosIG · acropolisIG · parthenonIG · athensFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

The headline is the Acropolis: a combined site ticket is about €30 adult in summer; children under 5 are free and non-EU kids 6–25 pay a reduced ~€15, so a family of four runs roughly €75–90 (~$85–100). Since 2024 entry is a mandatory timed slot — book online at hhticket.gr and arrive in your 15-minute window. The separate Acropolis Museum is ~€15 (summer) and is the air-conditioned, kid-friendly companion.

City heat

Hot, dry, sunny inland. June highs average ~30°C / 86°F and there is almost no shade on the rock. Go at opening (~8am) or after 6pm; the midday climb is brutal for an 8-year-old. Carry 2+ liters of water, hats, and sunscreen; sunset is ~9pm, so a late-afternoon visit stays comfortable.

Save Book the earliest timed slot to beat the heat and the cruise crowds, and skip the paid guided tours — the free hilltop climb to Areopagus or Filopappou gives the postcard Parthenon view for nothing.
Splurge Pair the site with the Acropolis Museum (the AC and the scale models are a genuine kid win), then a rooftop dinner in Plaka or Monastiraki with the lit Parthenon overhead.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Tzitzikas kai Mermigas (Syntagma) — big Greek menu, famous fresh chips and grilled-pork pita the kids reliably eat
  • A Plaka pizza/pasta trattoria — wood-fired pizza and simple pasta a two-minute walk from the site for the pickiest eater
  • A family taverna at the foot of the Acropolis — pork or chicken gyros, tzatziki, bread, and fries as the dependable fallback
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Late-afternoon Acropolis instead of morning if you want the marble in warm light and cooler air.
  • Acropolis Museum first on a scorching day for the AC, then the rock at 6pm.
  • Cape Sounion (Temple of Poseidon) as a half-day sea-cliff sunset drive if you have a spare afternoon.

Wed - 6/9/2027

Ferry to Naxos, Portara sunset

Into the Cyclades

Est. $170 · ferry, taxi, dinner

SleepNaxos (Chora) · night 1 of 4
TransferPiraeus -> Naxos high-speed ferry ~3h15 (SeaJets/Blue Star)
PayoffSunset through the marble Portara your first evening

Morning high-speed ferry from Piraeus out to Naxos, the greenest and most kid-friendly Cyclade. Collect the rental car, settle into Chora, and walk out to the Portara for sunset — the great marble doorway framing the sun over the Aegean.

Travel day - Piraeus to Naxos by ferry.

Naxos Chora & the Portara at sunset

PhotosIG · portaraIG · naxosIG · naxoschoraFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Free, 24/7. The Portara — the colossal marble doorway of an unfinished 6th-century-BC Temple of Apollo — stands on the islet off Naxos Town, a 10-minute walk across the causeway and up ~75 steps. It frames the sunset like nothing else in the Cyclades. Below it, Naxos Chora tumbles up to a Venetian kastro of stone lanes.

Aegean

Warm, sunny, breezy. June air tops out around ~28°C / 82°F with low humidity, and the sea has warmed to ~22°C / 72°F. Afternoons can pick up a north breeze (the meltemi is mild in early/mid June); the causeway to the Portara is exposed and glorious at golden hour.

Save The Portara is free — make it your first-evening ritual, arriving 45 minutes before sunset for a spot on the marble. Wander the kastro lanes for nothing rather than paying for a walking tour.
Splurge A waterfront-taverna dinner in Chora after the sunset, then a Naxos kitron (citron) liqueur nightcap for the grown-ups.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Caya (Naxos Town) — Italian-Mediterranean with wood-fired pizza and gnocchi, an easy picky-kid win
  • Scirocco (Protodikeiou Sq.) — family-run since 1995, modern Greek with pasta and grilled plates
  • Trata (Agios Georgios beach) — classic beach taverna: grilled meat, fries, pasta, calamari
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Portara at dawn if you want it crowd-free instead of the sunset scrum.
  • Kastro walk + Della Rocca-Barozzi tower for a bit of Venetian history in the lanes.
  • Naxos Town harbor stroll for a low-key first evening if the flight day was long.

Thu - 6/10/2027

Naxos beach day

Agios Prokopios & Plaka sand

Est. $150 · sunbeds, lunch, dinner

SleepNaxos (Chora) · night 2 of 4
AnchorAgios Prokopios + Plaka beaches (shallow, sandy)
WaterSea ~22°C / 72°F - the warmest, easiest swim of the trip

The reason Naxos is the base: long crescents of golden sand and gentle shallow water at Agios Prokopios and Plaka — the best family swimming on the trip. Swim in the calm morning, and keep the south beaches in your pocket if the afternoon breeze picks up.

Agios Prokopios & Plaka: the long sandy beaches

PhotosIG · agiosprokopiosIG · plakabeachIG · naxosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

The reason Naxos is the family base. Agios Prokopios is a Blue-Flag, 1.5 km crescent of golden sand with a gentle shallow shelf — the most kid-friendly swim on the trip. Sunbed-and-umbrella pairs run ~€20/day at the organized south end; the north end is free. Plaka beach next door is a 4 km ribbon of white sand and dunes. Water is a warming ~22°C / 72°F.

Sea

Sandy, shallow, west-facing. Warm sun and calm mornings; the meltemi, when it blows, hits these west-coast beaches strongest in the afternoon, so swim early and picnic late. For a truly wind-sheltered day, the south/east beaches (Agiassos, Kalantos) stay calmer.

Save Park at the free north end of Agios Prokopios, bring your own umbrella, and swim before the afternoon breeze; a local bus (~€3.50) reaches the beaches if you skip the car that day.
Splurge A front-row sunbed set with a beach-bar lunch, or a lesson at a Plaka/Mikri Vigla watersports center for the 13-year-old (windsurf/SUP).

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Beach-bar tavernas at Agios Prokopios — toasties, fries, pasta, and grilled fish steps from the sand
  • Caya or a Chora pizzeria — back in town for a reliable wood-fired-pizza dinner
  • Naxos Town gyros counters — cheap, fast picky-kid lunches between swims
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Plaka beach dunes for a wilder, quieter stretch than Agios Prokopios.
  • Mikri Vigla for the windier watersports end if the teen wants a lesson.
  • Agiassos or Kalantos (south) as the meltemi-sheltered backup on a windy day.

Naxos mountain villages

Halki, kitron & marble Apeiranthos

Est. $130 · car day, tastings, taverna lunch

SleepNaxos (Chora) · night 3 of 4
AnchorHalki (Vallindras kitron) + Apeiranthos marble village
WhyCooler, greener inland - a break from beach heat

Naxos is the one Cyclade with a real mountainous interior. Drive up through the Tragea olive valleys to Halki for a free citron-liqueur tasting, then marble-paved Apeiranthos — a cooler, greener half-day and a genuine change of pace from the sand.

Self-drive day around inland Naxos.

Halki & Apeiranthos: the mountain villages

PhotosIG · apeiranthosIG · halkiIG · naxosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Naxos is the greenest, most mountainous Cyclade, and its inland villages are the antidote to a pure beach trip. Halki (~25 min up) is the old kitron capital: the free Vallindras distillery pours a family-friendly citron-liqueur tasting with a small museum. Apeiranthos (~30–40 min) is a marble-paved mountain village of Venetian towers and little museums. Both are free to wander; you just need the rental car and a couple of euros for coffee.

Mountains

Cooler and greener up high. The drive climbs through Tragea olive valleys to villages several degrees cooler than the coast — a smart midday escape from beach heat or an afternoon meltemi. Marble lanes are shaded; bring grippy shoes for the cobbles.

Save The villages cost nothing to explore and the Vallindras kitron tasting is free — make it a self-drive loop (Halki → Filoti → Apeiranthos) with a taverna lunch, no tour needed.
Splurge Lunch at a mountain taverna in Apeiranthos or Filoti with a view over the Tragea, plus a stop at the Fish & Olive ceramics gallery in Halki.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Apeiranthos / Filoti mountain tavernas — grilled meat, fries, and local cheese with a valley view
  • Halki cafes on the square — toasties, sweets, and drinks around the kitron distillery
  • A bakery in Chalki or Filoti — cheese pies and pastries for a picky-kid car lunch
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Temple of Demeter (Sangri) as an easy ancient-site add-on on the way up.
  • Melanes / Flerio kouros to hunt the giant unfinished marble statue lying in an orchard.
  • Mount Zas cave for a short hike with the teen if you want a leg-stretch.

Sun - 6/13/2027

Ferry to Paros, Naoussa

Short hop, prettiest harbor

Est. $150 · ferry, taxi, harbor dinner

SleepParos (Naoussa/Parikia) · night 1 of 2
TransferNaxos -> Paros high-speed ferry ~30-40 min
PayoffNaoussa fishing harbor at golden hour

The easiest transfer of the trip: a 30–40 minute ferry hop to Paros. Base in or near Naoussa, the Cyclades’ loveliest fishing village — a working harbor, a half-sunk Venetian fort, and lanes that glow at sunset.

Travel day - Naxos to Paros by ferry.

Naoussa: the fishing harbor at golden hour

PhotosIG · naoussaIG · parosIG · naoussaparosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Free to soak up. Naoussa is the prettiest fishing village in the Cyclades: a working harbor of caiques and octopus drying on the quay, a half-sunk 15th-century Venetian kastro you can wade to, and a warren of white lanes that fill with tavernas at night. Golden hour (~18:30–19:30 in June) lights the boats and the fort — the trip’s most romantic evening, and it costs nothing but dinner.

Coast

Warm, bright, breezy. June air ~28°C / 82°F, sea ~22°C / 72°F. Paros is a windsurf island for a reason — the afternoon meltemi is real here — but Naoussa’s harbor and lanes are sheltered and calm at dusk.

Save Wander the harbor and kastro for free, wade out to the ruined fort, and eat gyros on the quay rather than a full sit-down if the budget is tight.
Splurge A seafood dinner on the Naoussa waterfront at sunset, then a gelato walk through the lantern-lit back lanes.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Pita Frank (Naoussa) — legendary ~€3.50 gyros, customizable, an instant kid favorite
  • Taverna Glafkos (Naoussa) — family-run, pasta and traditional plates on the water
  • Cuore Rosso (Parikia) — widely rated the island’s best pizza for the pickiest eater
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Parikia old town + Panagia Ekatontapyliani (the 4th-century "church of 100 doors") for a quieter afternoon.
  • Lefkes mountain village for a stone-lane, bougainvillea half-day inland.
  • Naoussa kastro swim off the rocks by the harbor fort.

Ferry to Milos, Plaka sunset

The volcanic finale begins

Est. $170 · ferry, car, dinner

SleepMilos (Adamas/Pollonia) · night 1 of 3
TransferParos -> Milos ferry ~2-2.5h (Blue Star)
PayoffKlima syrmata + Plaka castle at sunset

Ferry southwest to Milos, the volcanic showpiece. Collect the car, settle in Adamas or Pollonia, and ease in with the island’s villages: the color-doored syrmata of Klima and a sunset climb to the castle above whitewashed Plaka.

Travel day - Paros to Milos by ferry.

Plaka, Klima & the fishing coves

PhotosIG · klimaIG · plakamilosIG · firopotamosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

The island’s village-and-cove side. Klima is a row of syrmata — two-level fishermen’s houses with brightly painted doors carved right into the rock at the waterline, glowing at sunset. Hilltop Plaka is the whitewashed capital, with a free 200-step climb to a Venetian castle for one of the Aegean’s best sunsets. Firopotamos and Papafragas are tiny, photogenic swim coves. All free; you just need the car.

Villages

Warm evenings, west-facing sunsets. Klima and the Plaka castle both face west for the sunset; June nights are balmy for a village dinner. Papafragas has a steep, rocky final descent to the water — sturdy shoes, and mind the little ones.

Save Klima, Plaka, and the castle are all free — make a sunset loop of them, park once and walk, and swim at Firopotamos rather than a paid beach.
Splurge Sunset dinner in Plaka after the castle climb, or a couple of nights based in pretty Pollonia at the quieter north end.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Plaka / Trypiti tavernas — grilled plates and pasta with the sunset view
  • Adamas: Egoist & Gyros of Milos — burgers, pasta, and gyros for the picky crew
  • Pollonia seafront — relaxed pizza-and-seafood dinners at the north end
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Plaka castle at sunset for the island’s cinematic view (200 steps up).
  • Mandrakia — another photogenic syrmata fishing hamlet if Klima is busy.
  • Firopotamos swim as the calm, sandy-ish north-coast cove.

Tue - 6/15/2027

Kleftiko boat day

Sea caves by boat ·

Est. $560 · full-day family cruise

SleepMilos (Adamas/Pollonia) · night 2 of 3
AnchorFull-day Kleftiko cruise (book early in the stay)

The marquee: a full-day boat to Kleftiko’s white sea caves and arches, with swim and snorkel stops along the roadless southwest coast. Keep it flexible — if the wind is up, this is the day that slides into the buffer.

Kleftiko: the sea caves by boat

PhotosIG · kleftikoIG · milosIG · kleftikomilosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

The trip’s marquee: Kleftiko — a cathedral of white volcanic sea caves, arches, and stacks on Milos’s roadless southwest coast — is reachable only by boat. A full-day group cruise from Adamas or Pollonia runs ~€120–175/person (kids often reduced) and typically includes several swim/snorkel stops, gear, and lunch on board; a family of four is roughly €400–620 (~$450–680). Operators include Horizon, Excellent Yachting, Polco Sailing, and Zephyros.

Boat day

The wind-dependent day — the one to keep flexible. Kleftiko faces the open southwest, so a strong meltemi can force operators to cancel or reroute at short notice. June is the calm shoulder (the meltemi peaks in July–August), and cancellations are uncommon, but this is exactly why the plan carries buffer days: book it early in the Milos stay so a blown-out day can slide.

Save Take a larger group boat rather than a private charter, book directly with a Milos operator, and pick a morning departure when the sea is calmest.
Splurge A small-group or semi-private sailing catamaran to Kleftiko with fewer people, more swim time, and a proper on-board lunch.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Lunch is aboard — most full-day Kleftiko cruises feed you on the boat, so this is a packed day
  • Adamas waterfront on return — Aktaion for pizza/pasta or a gyros counter for the kids
  • Pollonia tavernas — if you sail from the north end, an easy seafood-or-pasta dinner after
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Half-day Kleftiko motorboat tour if a full 9-hour day is too much for the 8-year-old.
  • Reschedule within the Milos stay if the wind is up — the built-in reason for three nights.
  • Papafragas + Sarakiniko by car as the no-boat backup if Kleftiko blows out entirely.

Blue Lagoon + Comino

Cost

June weather
