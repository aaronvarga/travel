Portugal 9 live

Compare 9 live Portugal plans head-to-head.

How these differ

Every Portugal plan opens on the Portuguese side — Cascais and Lisbon, the Algarve, and/or Madeira — so the real choice is the warm-water second leg, or whether to have one at all. Portugal Algarve & Madeira stays entirely Portuguese: the best value and the fewest transfers, but the Atlantic runs cool for swimming. Adding a Mediterranean leg buys warm water at higher cost and a little more travel friction — Mallorca for sandy coves and the easiest swimming, Crete for warm Aegean beaches and Venetian towns, or Sicily for the strongest food and Mount Etna (but the most fragile flights and the lowest ease). The three-stop versions (Algarve + Sicily, Madeira + Sicily) pack in the most but carry the most transfers, and Greece via Lisbon is really a Crete trip with Lisbon and Athens as short cultural bookends.

Italy 8 live · 1 excluded

Compare 8 live Italy plans head-to-head.

How these differ

The Italian plans divide by which Italy you want. Sicily anchors most of them — Cefalù, Taormina, Mount Etna, baroque Ortigia — and brings the board's best food; Sicily & Malta adds Malta's limestone cities and brilliant blue swimming. Sardinia & Corsica trades food and monuments for the best pure swimming: turquoise coves and boat days. Venice, Dolomites & Sardinia is the outlier — high-alpine hiking into a warm-water Sardinia finish, the strongest mountain-to-beach contrast on the board. The rest pair Sicily with a non-Italian opener (Portugal, Madeira, or the Swiss Alps), so the Italian half stays similar while the first week — and the price and flight risk — change. Ease is low across all of them; these are ambitious, transfer-heavy trips.

Greece 7 live · 1 excluded

Compare 7 live Greece plans head-to-head.

How these differ

All the Greek plans swim in warm Aegean or Ionian water, so the split is about vibe and logistics, not temperature. Greece: Ionian Islands is the calmest and easiest — Lefkada and Kefalonia are road- or short-ferry-connected, it stays in one country, and ferry stress is minimal — but it's the least varied. Greek Cyclades is the postcard version: Naxos, Paros and Milos reached by inter-island ferries out of Athens — more iconic and more varied, but exposed to June's meltemi wind and with more moving parts. The other four all center on Crete's Chania coast and differ only in the opener bolted on the front — Portugal's cities, Madeira's levada hiking, the Swiss Alps, or a light Lisbon-and-Athens culture bookend. Crete itself gives the warmest, most family-friendly beaches; the opener is what sets the price, the pace, and how much scenic contrast you get (the Alps opener is the priciest and coldest-starting; Portugal or Madeira add a full hiking or city week; Lisbon keeps it lightest).

How these differ

Both Hawaii plans deliver warm swimming and snorkeling; they differ on islands, length, and cost. How these differ

Both Swiss plans run the identical five-night Alps opener — Jungfraujoch, the Grindelwald-First cliff walk, Lauterbrunnen's waterfalls — then split on the warm-water finish. Switzerland + Sicily ends with food, baroque towns, and Mount Etna; Switzerland + Crete ends with calmer Aegean swimming on Crete's Chania coast. Sicily is the richer, busier second week; Crete is the simpler, warmer-water one. Both sit among the priciest plans because of the Alps.
