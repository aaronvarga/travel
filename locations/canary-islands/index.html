The Plan at a Glance

Madeira ecology, easier hub

This is the Macaronesian Laurisilva + volcano epic option: Tenerife for Teide, Anaga, Masca, and beach recovery; La Palma for Caldera de Taburiente, Los Tilos, Ruta de los Volcanes, and Roque de los Muchachos. The route is deliberately built to end before the Jun 23-26 Pittsburgh blackout.

Route

PIT -> Tenerife -> La Palma -> Tenerife -> PIT

6 nights Tenerife, 5 nights La Palma, then 1 Tenerife airport buffer. Home by Tue Jun 22.

Why it beats Madeira on flights

Tenerife is a major hub; Funchal is a funnel

Tenerife South has broad direct European service and Tenerife North has frequent inter-island hops. Madeira/Funchal is excellent, but US routings more often funnel through Lisbon and its weather-sensitive airport.

Budget

Priced target: ~$12,420; high case ~$14,690

Under the $15k hard cap if economy airfare stays near $1,000-$1,150 per person and lodging stays apartment-first.

Why This Trip

Madeira-style island ecology

Pick the Canaries if the appeal is weird island landscapes: Teide, Anaga, black sand, La Palma caldera country, Los Tilos, volcano ridges, and ocean towns.

Choose this if

  • Variety 5 and Novelty 5 are the draw: two volcanic islands, laurisilva forests, high ridges, black-sand beaches, and starry observatory country.
  • Tenerife gives the larger air and beach infrastructure; La Palma gives the quieter ecological payoff Madeira fans are chasing.
  • The route protects the Jun 23-26 blackout and uses 12 nights, with family-scaled versions of the serious La Palma hikes.

Reasons to pause

  • Budget is only 2 and Ease 2: the $11.8k-$14.7k range leaves little cushion if flights price high.
  • Swim 3 means Atlantic water around 70-72 F; good for dips, not the warmest kid-swim option.
  • La Palma is quieter and less resort-built; if a flight gets ugly, Gran Canaria may be the practical backup but is less distinctive.

Kids’ Favorites

Kid-favorite moments

Not every excellent stop makes this list. These are the clearest kid-facing payoffs from the full itinerary.

  • Teide National Park - volcano landscapes that feel like another planet.
  • Las Teresitas and black-sand beaches - easy beach contrast without overthinking the day.
  • Caldera de Taburiente - big canyon scenery with routes that can be scaled.
  • Roque de los Muchachos - observatories, clouds below you, and a true high-point moment.

Thu · Jun 10

Teide National Park

Spain highest peak + real trail segment

Est. $290 · cable car, snacks, dinner

SleepPuerto de la Cruz / north Tenerife · night 2 of 6
Drive~1h20-1h45 each way depending base
AnchorCable car + Roques de Garcia / lower trail

This is the volcano-epic opener: cable car if wind allows, but a real on-foot lava landscape segment either way.

Teide National Park: cable car + Roques de Garcia trail segment

PhotosIG · teideIG · teidenationalparkIG · tenerifeFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Teide cable car official 2026 price signal: non-resident adult return about €42, child 3-13 return about €21. Family cable-car baseline: about €126 before parking, food, summit permits, or a guided add-on.

Altitude

3,715m peak / high desert. Tenerife coast can be upper 70s F in June, but the La Rambleta top station is 3,555m and colder, sunnier, windier, and dehydration-prone. Closed mountain/sports shoes are required; no under-3s on the cable car.

Save Buy cable-car tickets direct, skip the summit-permit obsession unless everyone is fit, and do a real but short trail: a top-station viewpoint trail, Roques de Garcia segment, or Montana Blanca lower section.
Splurge A sunrise/stargazing guided Teide evening works if everyone handles altitude, but it is not required for the family win.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Da Arianna, Puerto de la Cruz - fresh pasta and pizza after the mountain
  • Restaurante Divino - chicken nuggets/fries, wings, and pasta fallback
  • Pack lunch - Teide services are limited and mountain timing is better with snacks in the car
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Roques de Garcia only if the cable car is wind-closed or anyone feels altitude effects.
  • Mirador de Chipeque for an easier sunset view if Teide tickets sell out.
  • La Orotava old town as the low-altitude fallback in bad mountain weather.

Anaga laurel forest: Cruz del Carmen + Sendero de los Sentidos

PhotosIG · anagaIG · laurisilvaIG · tenerifeFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Most Anaga walking is free. The no-drama family anchor is Sendero de los Sentidos from Cruz del Carmen: about 2.15 km, low difficulty, with accessible boardwalk sections on route 1. El Pijaral / Enchanted Forest requires advance permission and strict caps.

Forest

Cloud forest microclimate. It can be misty, wet, and 10-15 F cooler than Santa Cruz. Bring a light rain shell even when the beach forecast looks perfect.

Save Use the free visitor-center trails and viewpoints. This is the Madeira-style Laurisilva day without paying for a guide.
Splurge Hire a private Anaga naturalist if you want the forest ecology interpreted instead of just photographed.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • La Laguna family restaurants - best post-forest dinner base if you stay east/north
  • Bar-cafe fallback near Cruz del Carmen - simple sandwiches/drinks, but do not rely on a full picky-kid meal in the forest
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Taganana viewpoint loop if roads and stomachs are holding up.
  • Playa de Benijo for a black-sand scenery add-on, not a safe calm swim default.
  • San Cristobal de La Laguna if fog/rain makes hiking unpleasant.

Masca / Teno decision day

Gorge drama without forcing a risky kid hike

Est. $220-$430 · depends on gorge tickets/boat

SleepPuerto de la Cruz / north Tenerife · night 5 of 6
Drive~1h30-2h each way plus mountain-road time
RuleFull Masca gorge only if booked and kid-ready

Masca is spectacular, but the official gorge is high difficulty. The default family plan is village, viewpoints, and a short legal walk; full descent is a conscious upgrade.

Masca gorge decision day

PhotosIG · mascaIG · barrancodemascaIG · tenerifeFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Official Masca trail booking currently lists descent-only access from about €40.66 adult and reduced youth pricing, with helmet, hiking-shoe, water, ID, boat-ticket, and bus-access rules. Family baseline: treat full-gorge hiking as optional, not automatic.

Trail

Dry, rocky, exposed. The official descent is about 5 km one-way / ~4 hours and high difficulty. Under-8s are not allowed; an 8-year-old is legal but borderline unless already a strong hiker.

Save Do Masca village, viewpoints, and a short legal walk instead of buying four full gorge tickets.
Splurge If everyone is trail-ready, book the official descent and a boat/transfer plan; do not improvise this hike.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Masca village restaurants - scenic but limited; bring kid snacks
  • Santiago del Teide fallback - simpler post-road food
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Los Gigantes boat if you want cliff drama without a hard canyon day.
  • Teno lighthouse / Buenavista if road access and timing line up.
  • Garachico for a lava-pool town alternative if Masca permits/weather fail.

Caldera de Taburiente: Los Brecitos / La Cumbrecita family version

PhotosIG · calderadetaburienteIG · lapalmaIG · losbrecitosFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

National park entry is free. Classic Los Brecitos logistics usually mean parking near Barranco de las Angustias and paying an authorized 4x4 taxi uphill; budget about €50-60 family vehicle, uncertain until rechecked.

Trail

Long rocky day if done classic. The official Los Brecitos descent is about 12.5 km / 6 hr and high difficulty. With an 8-year-old, keep La Cumbrecita / visitor-center / partial-valley options alive.

Save Use La Cumbrecita viewpoints and a shorter marked walk if taxi timing or kid energy looks bad.

  • Visitor center + short barranco walk for a lower-risk Caldera day.
  • Tazacorte beach sunset after the hike if legs are still functioning.

Entry & Documents

Passports and essentials

Documents, driving, money, health, and connectivity for this trip. Time-sensitive items (especially ETIAS) can change — re-check the linked official sources close to departure.

Spain (incl. Mallorca)

PassportValid ≥3 months beyond your planned EU departure, and issued within the last 10 years.
ETIASExpected to launch late 2026 (re-check before June 2027). — €20; under-18s and over-70s exempt.
VisaNone for US citizens — visa-free up to 90 days per 180.
Driving permitRecommended with a US license for rentals; carry passport, license, IDP, and rental paperwork together.

TollsNo island toll-road system like mainland Portugal; budget for parking, blue-zone meters, and possible beach/natural-park lots.

Travel Tips

The stuff nobody tells you

Operational rules that keep this from becoming a beautiful but fragile route.

Book in roughly this order

  1. Protected PIT-Tenerife long-haul routing · start tracking when schedules load
  2. Refundable Tenerife and La Palma apartments · parking, AC, washer, real beds
  3. Two separate rental cars · automatic if needed; do not ferry without written permission
  4. Teide cable car and Masca decision · book only once weather/fitness plan is honest
  5. La Palma Caldera/Roque logistics · taxi, parking, visitor center, tour windows

  • Flights

    Use Tenerife as the air machine

    • Prefer TFS for long-haul Europe connections if fare/schedule is better.
    • Use TFN for La Palma. It is the practical inter-island airport.
    • Reposition before flying home. SPC homebound is the fragile part.

  • Hiking

    Scale the adult trails

    • Masca, Caldera, and Ruta are not casual kid walks at full length.
    • Carry layers and sun protection for Teide/Roque altitude.
    • Turn around early when the day is still fun.

  • Food

    Keep easy dinners close

    • Puerto de la Cruz, La Laguna, and Los Llanos are the safe dinner bases.
    • Pack lunches for high-mountain days; summit services are thin.
    • Use pizza/pasta fallbacks without guilt after hard hikes.

  • Beach expectations

    Atlantic, not Mediterranean

    • Water is swimmable but not bath-warm.
    • Las Teresitas is the calm family beach.
    • Black-sand beaches can be rougher. Treat Benijo/Bollullo as scenic unless conditions are clearly safe.

What to bring

  • Passports / IDs + 2 photocopies stored separately (see the Entry & Documents section)
  • Debit + 2 credit cards (call issuers re: travel); some local cash on arrival

  • Small first-aid kit: pain/fever meds (adult + kids), band-aids, motion-sickness tabs, any prescriptions in original bottles
  • Sunscreen (SPF 50), after-sun, insect repellent
  • Light day pack + a foldable tote for beach/market runs
  • Copies of bookings (flights, lodging, car, tickets) saved offline

Hiking & levada days

  • Broken-in trail shoes / light hikers for everyone
  • Daypack with rain shell — mountain weather turns fast
  • Headlamp (Madeira levada tunnels are dark and wet)
  • Trekking layers; blister plasters

Beach & water days

  • Swimsuits (2 each), quick-dry towels, rash guards for the kids
  • Water shoes — many Mediterranean beaches are pebble/rock
  • Goggles + a mesh bag for wet gear
  • Dry bag for phones/keys on boat days

Hot-weather (June sun)

  • Sun hats and UV sunglasses for all
  • Light, breathable long sleeves for midday sun protection
  • Electrolyte tabs — easy to under-hydrate with kids in the heat
  • Plan indoor/shade breaks 1–4pm

Rain / changeable weather

  • Packable rain jackets over umbrellas (wind)
  • A spare set of dry clothes in the day pack
  • Ziplocs to protect electronics

This trip specifically

  • Altitude layers: fleece or light puffer for Teide and Roque even in June.
  • Real hiking shoes: volcanic rock is rough on casual sneakers.
  • Sun management: UPF shirts, hats, sunglasses, sunscreen, and electrolyte tabs.
  • Offline trail maps: download Teide, Anaga, Caldera, Los Tilos, and El Pilar areas.
  • Car kit: nausea meds, snacks, spare water, and a light towel for black-sand beaches.

What People Are Saying

Research signals to keep in mind

Official pages and current route/fare sources point to the same conclusion: La Palma is the better nature island, Tenerife is the better air gateway.

  • Connectivity signal

    Tenerife South is the big international gateway, Tenerife North is the inter-island workhorse, and La Palma is thin but reachable. That is why the route enters and exits through Tenerife.

  • Nature signal

    Los Tilos, Caldera, Ruta de los Volcanes, and Roque de los Muchachos are the exact reasons not to replace La Palma with an easier resort island unless flight routing forces it.

  • Family signal

    The best version is not the hardest version. Use family segments of big trails, protect beach recovery, and avoid stacking three high-effort hikes in a row.
