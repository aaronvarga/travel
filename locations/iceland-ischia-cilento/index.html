Why This Pairing

The contrast is the feature

This is not two half-trips stapled together; each half fixes the other’s weakness.

What Iceland contributes

Four nights with a buffer

  • Waterfalls, black sand, geysers and geothermal pools.
  • A fourth night lets the forecast choose the big-loop order.
  • Reykjanes stays optional when both flagship loops succeed.

What Campania contributes

Seven warm-water nights

  • Three Forio nights protect Castello and Negombo.
  • Four Cilento nights keep quieter family sand, Paestum and Palinuro.
  • Pizza, pasta, burgers and fries remain available every day.

The honest trade

Better Iceland, tighter Ischia

  • The 6/15/2027 flight–train–hydrofoil sequence needs real buffer.
  • La Mortella is folded into the Forio sunset plan or dropped if the castle runs long.
  • The final FCO night remains resilience, with Rome only as an early-arrival option.

Kids’ Favorites

The moments most likely to win ages 13 and 8

Big visual payoffs, frequent water and easy food are deliberately spaced through both halves.

Water that feels different

Pools, bays and caves

  • Reykjavík’s heated municipal pools after the overnight flight.
  • Secret Lagoon after geysers and waterfalls.
  • San Montano’s sheltered warm sea plus Negombo’s volcanic pools.
  • A Palinuro boat that enters sea caves when conditions cooperate.

Real-world adventure

Not another museum march

  • Walk behind Seljalandsfoss and feel the spray at Skógafoss.
  • Cross the causeway and climb into Castello Aragonese.
  • Boat, swim and explore instead of spending whole days sightseeing indoors.
  • Shorten or skip any climb without losing the day’s main payoff.

The food safety net

Simple choices every day

  • Pizza and plain pasta throughout Ischia and Cilento.
  • Burgers, fries and supermarkets in Reykjavík.
  • Packed road lunches for Iceland and Palinuro.
  • Gelato, beach snacks and apartment breakfasts reduce restaurant fatigue.
Best bets: the 8-year-old gets geothermal pools, protected bays and cave boats; the 13-year-old gets dramatic waterfalls, the castle climb, black sand and stronger photography moments. Both get a beach or water payoff after the culture-heavy stops.

Iceland

PassportValid ≥3 months beyond your planned Schengen departure, and issued within the last 10 years. Iceland is in the Schengen Area, so time here counts against the 90/180 clock.
ETIASExpected to launch late 2026 (repeatedly delayed — not yet confirmed). Applies to Iceland as a Schengen state. — €20 (confirmed Jul 2025, subject to change; under-18s and over-70s exempt).
VisaNone for US citizens — visa-free up to 90 days per 180.
Driving permitA US license is accepted for short stays; an IDP is recommended (cheap translation) and some rental insurers ask for it. Carry passport, license, IDP, and rental paperwork together.
Rental carsDrive on the right; rentals default to automatic (still book early for June). Min age ~20 (under-25 surcharge common). The SW/South route needs no F-roads or 4x4, but roads are windy, gravel shoulders are loose, and single-lane bridges (einbreið brú) require yielding. Headlights must be ON 24/7 (midnight sun makes this easy to forget). Off-road/off-track driving is illegal and heavily fined. Hold the car door against the wind — wind damage is the #1 rental claim, and gravel/sand-ash (SAAP/GP) insurance is worth it on the south coast.
TollsEffectively toll-free on this route. The Hvalfjörður tunnel (near Reykjavík) is now free; the only toll road is Vaðlaheiðargöng in the north (not on this itinerary, paid online within 3 hours if used).
MoneyIceland is nearly cashless — contactless cards work everywhere, including gas pumps, parking, and remote guesthouses. A card with a PIN is essential for unattended fuel pumps. You barely need any. Currency is the Icelandic króna (ISK), ~135–140 ISK per USD. Skip the currency exchange; a fee-free card covers everything.
TippingNot expected or customary — service is included. Rounding up for exceptional service is welcome but never required.
Tap waterTap water is glacial, free, and among the best on Earth — always drink from the tap, never buy bottled. Cold tap is odourless; HOT tap water is geothermal and smells faintly of sulfur (rotten-egg) — that's normal and harmless, just use cold for drinking.
Emergency112 (single number for all emergencies; the 112 Iceland app can send your GPS location to rescue teams — install it).
ConnectivityAiralo, Nova, or Síminn eSIMs work well; buy before departure. Coverage is strong along the whole SW/South ring-road corridor; expect dead zones only in the highlands (not on this route).
ETIAS is a pre-travel authorization for visa-free visitors incl. US citizens. Timeline has slipped several times — confirm on the official EU site close to departure. If it isn't yet live for Jun 2027, no action is needed.
