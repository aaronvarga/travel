The Plan at a Glance

One open-jaw flight, no backtracking, zero overlap with the last Puglia trip

Every stop below is new ground — Salento runs south of the Monopoli beaches you already saw, and Maratea/Sorrento are coasts you haven't touched at all.

Route

PIT → BDS (Brindisi) ⇢ drive south & west ⇢ NAP (Naples) → PIT

Open-jaw flight avoids driving back across the width of Italy; one rental car, picked up in Brindisi and dropped in Naples.

4 + 3 + 5 nights

Plan Health-Check

What to lock, watch, and expect

Researched July 2026 for a June 2027 trip; kids will be 13 and 8.

Act now

Book the open-jaw flight the moment 2027 schedules open

An open-jaw ticket (into Brindisi, out of Naples) prices like one round-trip, ~$1,300-1,800/person in peak June — not two, so budget ~$5.2-7.2k for the family, not the doubled figure. June is peak and Brindisi has thin capacity; 2027 schedules open ~11 months out, which is right now.

Act now

Reserve an automatic-transmission one-way rental 3-4 months out

Automatics are genuinely scarce in Italy and a one-way Brindisi→Naples drop narrows inventory further. Also arrange an International Driving Permit through AAA before you leave — Italy doesn't recognize a US license alone.

Heads-up

Juneteenth's Friday-observance is employer-dependent

June 19, 2027 falls on a Saturday, so it's federally observed Friday June 18 — which lands on this trip's Maratea→Sorrento transfer day. That's guaranteed for federal employees but not for private employers — confirm with HR before counting on it.

Heads-up

Gallipoli → Maratea is a genuine 4h20m cross-country transfer

There's no direct autostrada between Adriatic Puglia and Tyrrhenian Basilicata. Avoid the coastal SS106 through Calabria — it has a documented poor safety record — and route inland via the toll-free A2 instead. Build in a real rest stop.

Solid — keep it

Ischia fits the kids better than a crowd-heavy island checklist

The island gives an 8- and 13-year-old beaches, boat taxis, castles, and casual pizza instead of a line-based attraction day. Thermal parks still have pool-specific age rules, so treat them as optional.

Solid — keep it

Only two travel days in twelve nights

The 3-base structure (Salento → Maratea → Sorrento) means just two driving transfers, each well under half a day — the rest is beach, town, and day-trip time, not car time.

Where We Are Staying

Three home bases, west across the boot

Each base was picked for one job: Gallipoli for Salento's best beaches, Maratea as a dramatic breather crossing the peninsula, Sorrento as flat, drivable home base for Ischia/Amalfi/Procida.

4 nights · Jun 11-14

Gallipoli

Arrival base — Salento

A small old town built on its own island, ringed by the beach clubs and free coves of Baia Verde. Everything in Salento — Otranto, Nardò, Punta Prosciutto — is under an hour's drive.

Book: A 2BR apartment inside or near the old town, not a hotel on the mainland side — walkable to Spiaggia della Purità.

3 nights · Jun 15-17

Maratea

Crossing the peninsula — Basilicata

Basilicata's one great coastal town: cliffs, a giant Christ statue with sweeping views, and hidden coves nothing like Salento's flat sand. A deliberate breather between two beach-heavy legs.

Book: A small hotel or apartment in Maratea centro or Acquafredda — inventory is thin, book 4-5 months out.

5 nights · Jun 18-22

Sorrento

Grand finale — Campania

Flatter and easier to drive/park than the cliffside Amalfi towns, with seasonal ferry access to Ischia and a manageable hop to the pastel island of Procida — the logistics base for the whole Amalfi Coast without living on a cliff.

Book: A central 2BR apartment or a hotel family suite — Sorrento is the tightest, priciest lodging market of the three bases, book 6+ months out.

Gallipoli

PhotosIG · GallipoliFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Beach clubs on Baia Verde run €20-40/day for umbrella + 2 sunbeds (peaks near €40 in August); the free public beach in the old town costs nothing.

Weather

Air 75°F day / 61°F night. Sea warms from ~70°F early June to ~73°F by month's end — comfortable, not warm yet.

Save Skip the paid lidos for Spiaggia della Purità, the free public beach tucked below the old town's walls — no rental fee, no parking cost.
Splurge Half-day boat trip to the sea caves and coves near Punta Pizzo/Santa Maria di Leuca, ~€30-45/person, with swim stops unreachable on foot.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Osteria Briganti — seafood osteria in the old town alleys, fresh catch and pasta
  • La Vinaigrette — seafront on the old town's Riviera, Salento seasonal dishes
  • La Pizza da Franco — wood-fired pizzeria, straightforward margherita for picky eaters
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Punta della Suina ↩ — pine-forest beach 4km south, nicknamed the "Caribbean of the Ionian," quieter than Baia Verde with both free and lido sections.
  • Torre Lapillo ↗ — same clear water as Punta Prosciutto with more restaurants and services nearby.
  • Nardo old town ↗ — authentic baroque lanes with fewer day-trippers than the headline towns.

Pescoluse (Le Maldive del Salento)

PhotosIG · PescoluseFlickr
1 / 1
Cost

Beach access is free; budget ~€20-40 for a lido umbrella/loungers if you want shade and bathrooms, plus a casual lunch. Parking is usually the only unavoidable extra.

Weather

Air ~80°F day / ~68°F night. Sea ~72°F — shallow sand and clear water make this one of the easiest kid swims in Salento.

Save Bring towels and use the free beach, then keep lunch simple with focaccia, fruit, and cold drinks from town.
Splurge Book a front-row umbrella at a lido so the day has shade, bathrooms, and no logistics beyond swimming and lunch.

Family & picky-kid eats

  • Maldive del Salento lido — beach bar/restaurant setup right on the sand when you want the easiest family lunch

  • Boteguita Beach Pub — casual beach food, snacks, and cold drinks close to the water
Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Nardò ↩ — about 25 minutes from Gallipoli, an authentic baroque old town with real local life and far fewer day-trippers.
  • Nardo old town ↗ — authentic baroque lanes with fewer day-trippers than the headline towns.

Otranto

Cost

Castello Aragonese: €3 adult / €2 ages 6-14. Cathedral (mosaic floor) is free. Lido sunbeds ~€20-30/day for two; free rocky bathing spots exist south of the old town.

Weather

Save Walk 10-15 minutes south along the seafront to Spiaggia dei Gradoni (Baia del Turco), a free rocky bathing spot with ladders into clear water.
Splurge Half-day boat excursion from the harbor up the coast to Baia dei Turchi and the sea caves of the Riviera degli Haethey, ~€35-85/person.

Family & picky-kid eats

Also awesome instead / if you have time
  • Torre dell'Orso ↩ — a golden-sand bay 20 minutes north framed by pine forest, famous for the "Due Sorelle" sea stacks just offshore.
  • Porto Selvaggio ↗ — pine-forest coastal reserve if the family wants nature over a lido.

Ischia

PhotosIG · IschiaFlickr
1 / 3
Cost

Direct seasonal ferry from Sorrento is roughly €24-30 adult one-way, with child fare around €18; family round trip is roughly €200-240 before luggage/fees. Add island bus/taxi money or a short boat taxi if Sant'Angelo/Maronti is the focus.

Weather

Save Choose one side of the island: Ischia Porto/Ponte plus Castello Aragonese, or Sant'Angelo plus Maronti. Trying both ends by taxi burns money and time.

Also awesome instead / if you have time

Save money

  • Skip Salento's paid lidos — Spiaggia della Purità (Gallipoli), Pescoluse's free beach, and the free stretch near Riva degli Angeli (Punta Prosciutto) cost nothing vs €20-40/day for a lounger.
  • Walk into the Grotta delle Meraviglie in Maratea instead of a private boat charter — €5/adult vs €120+ for a half-day charter.
  • Keep Ischia to one zone — Sant'Angelo/Maronti or Ischia Porto/Ponte — instead of taxiing across the island all day.
  • Book a mid-size SUV, not a 7-seat minivan — saves roughly €500-800 over 12 days for a family that doesn't need the extra seats.
  • Use Sorrento's basic sunbed rental (~€10-12) instead of a premium club like Peter's Beach (~€54/person) — saves ~€40+/person/day.

Worth the splurge

  • Private half-day boat charter along the Maratea coast (~€120+ for the boat) — sea caves and swim stops no road trip reaches.
  • Shared boat tour along the Amalfi Coast (~€79-95/person) instead of the carsickness-inducing SS163 hairpins — the single best fix for a carsick-prone kid on this trip.
  • Sunset aperitivo at Vista Skybar in Sorrento (€40/person minimum) — a full Bay of Naples and Vesuvius view to close out the trip.
  • Small boat loop around Procida — best if a seasonal return ferry lets you catch Marina Corricella in late light.
  • Half-day boat excursion from Gallipoli or Otranto to the sea caves and Baia dei Turchi (~€30-85/person) — the best water in Salento that's unreachable on foot.

Money & Tickets

Cash, kids, and confirmed prices

  • Book Ischia outbound and return together. The direct seasonal Sorrento route is thin; do not assume you can improvise the afternoon return.
  • Procida ferry timing matters more than tickets. Check whether the 2027 schedule gives you a direct seasonal hydrofoil or a clean Naples Beverello connection.
  • Amalfi Cathedral's ticket price is quoted as both €3 and €4 by different sources. Budget €4/adult and treat any change as a pleasant surprise.

Driving & Transfers

The one long day, done safely

  • Route Gallipoli→Maratea inland via the A2, not the coastal SS106. The Calabria stretch has a documented poor safety record — the inland autostrada is just as fast and safer.
  • Get an International Driving Permit from AAA before you leave. Italy doesn't recognize a US license alone — cheap and fast to get, but must be arranged stateside.
  • Pick up and drop off the rental car at the airport, not a city branch. Better automatic-transmission availability for a one-way Brindisi→Naples rental.
  • Budget 1.5 hours for Sorrento→Naples Airport on the fly-home day. Only 30 miles, but the Sorrentine Peninsula roads are narrow and congested near Vico Equense in summer.

Beaches & Boats

Skip lines, skip carsickness

  • Decide whether Ischia is a beach day or a thermal-park day before you go. Poseidon Gardens is a real spend; Sant'Angelo and Maronti can stay much cheaper.
  • Thermal parks have child and pool age rules. Use moderate family pools only and skip the hottest thermal pools with the 8-year-old.
  • Do the Amalfi Coast by shared boat, not rental car or SITA bus. The SS163 hairpins are a real carsickness risk — a shared boat tour (~€79-95/person) skips the road entirely.
  • The Grotta delle Meraviglie in Maratea is walk-in, not boat-only. €5/adult on foot vs €120+ for a private boat charter to see a real sea cave.

Food for Picky Eaters

A plain-pizza fallback at every base

  • Every base has a reliable margherita-pizza fallback. La Pizza da Franco (Gallipoli), Lu Tata N'zinu (Otranto), Il Graffio (Punta Prosciutto), Pizzeria da Franco (Sorrento) are all confirmed casual, kid-friendly spots.
  • Ask for pasta "in bianco" or "al pomodoro" anywhere, even nicer spots. Italian kitchens will almost always make a plain version off-menu — worth asking at Circolo dei Forestieri or Taverna Rovita rather than skipping them.
  • Da Gaetano on Ischia is the plain-pizza safety valve. Use it if the island day runs longer than planned and everyone just needs a low-friction meal.

Weather & Packing

Early-June water, midday sun

  • Sea temps run cooler in Salento/Maratea (~68-73°F) than Sorrento/Ischia/Amalfi (~72-74°F). Pack a light wetsuit top for the younger kid if they run cold — mid-June is still early season.
  • Procida is exposed between ferry waits, harbor walks, and the Terra Murata climb. Bring hats, water, and a flexible return plan in case the afternoon boat schedule shifts.
  • June is peak-pricing, not peak-crowd, season on this route. Prices run high but Salento/Maratea aren't yet at August capacity — Ischia and Amalfi will still feel busy regardless.
