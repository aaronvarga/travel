What is closed right now

This is the closure logic behind the route—not a promise that conditions will remain unchanged.

Primary corridor

SR 542 to Artist Point is open

WSDOT reopened the final 2.7 miles on June 10. Artist Point has vault toilets and garbage cans, but limited cell service and no potable water.

Current fire status

No listed closure on the planned Baker route

Checked Aug. 25: the Forest Service alert list does not close Artist Point, Heather Meadows, Chain Lakes or Ptarmigan Ridge. The Miner's Fire closure is in the Darrington Ranger District, away from this Highway 542 plan. Fire danger remains very high and a forest-wide campfire ban is active.

Closed side roads

Do not improvise toward Heliotrope

Glacier Creek Road is closed at mile 3 for bridge repairs, blocking vehicle access to Heliotrope Ridge. Middle Fork Nooksack Road is also closed at mile 7. Neither is needed here.

Trail condition gate

Open is not the same as guaranteed

Recheck the Forest Service alert list, WSDOT, AirNow and the actual ridge forecast the night before and again before leaving Glacier. A new closure, unhealthy AQI or obscured peaks cancels the alpine hike.

Mount Rainier update, re-read Aug. 31 (NPS pages updated Aug. 31): Sunrise Road and White River Road remain closed; all trails originating from those roads are closed, including Mount Fremont and Burroughs. The Plummer Peak Fire closure that briefly took Pinnacle Peak Trail (Aug. 24) was lifted — the NPS trail report cleared the Pinnacle row on Aug. 27 and the Aug. 31 alerts page lists only the north-side closures. Pinnacle Saddle, Skyline, Bench and Snow Lakes, Comet Falls, Van Trump Park, Eagle Peak and Naches Peak all read open, so the paid-Packwood Rainier trip keeps its original arrival hike.

Go / no-go

The rule for every mountain morning

The trip is allowed to become a lake, town or Seattle day. No lodging payment makes unhealthy air or an expanding closure worth pursuing.

Go

Open + clean air + useful visibility

All three must be true. Verify official alerts, AQI/smoke forecast and a nearby webcam.

Modify

Open but marginal

Use the shorter hike, overlooks and town time. Keep the car near a direct exit route.

Leave

Closure expands or air is unhealthy

Do not search for a back road. Move toward the alternate base or Seattle.

Recheck

Night before + before departure

Screenshot source pages only after the morning check; yesterday’s status is not today’s clearance.

Picture Lake + Artist Point

Photos

Exact flow

Rental around 11:30; quick lunch north of SEA; groceries and fuel in Bellingham or Maple Falls; Glacier check-in target 3:30–4:30; leave again by 5:00. Photograph Picture Lake first, then continue to Artist Point for the short Artist Ridge/Huntoon Point walk and sunset. Descend only with headlamps and an offline map.

The best complete Rainier-style day: volcano, jagged peak, meadows and alpine lakes

Chain Lakes Loop

Photos
1 / 7
Exact flow

Carry breakfast and leave before dawn. Start hiking around 6:30, taking the Artist Point–Herman Saddle–lakes circuit in the direction favored by current trail advice. Finish around noon, eat the packed lunch, then stop at Nooksack Falls only if the signed access and group energy are good. Return to Glacier for a genuine recovery afternoon.

Reality check

An open parking lot does not make a snow crossing safe. If snow, cloud or AQI fails the morning gate, use Bagley Lakes plus Picture Lake and keep the car close. Do not substitute a steeper unofficial line.

Cost / access Northwest Forest Pass or accepted interagency pass. There is no attraction ticket; carry water because Artist Point has none.
Food Wake ’N Bakery works only if you buy the prior day or its hours align; the hiking start is earlier than normal café service. Chair 9 provides pizza after the hike.

Ptarmigan Ridge

Photos
1 / 6
Exact flow

Use the same early Artist Point start. Follow Chain Lakes Trail to the signed Ptarmigan Ridge junction, cross only snow that is straightforward without mountaineering equipment, and turn around at the 3.8-mile viewpoint if the longer finish no longer adds value. Return to Glacier by midafternoon. Repeat Artist Ridge at sunset only if Friday failed and everyone genuinely wants it.

Reality check

This is rocky, exposed and almost entirely without shade. Two consecutive major hikes are optional, not a contract. The excellent shorter plan is Artist Ridge plus Bagley Lakes, keeping the day scenic without forcing nine miles on tired legs.

Cost / access Northwest Forest Pass or accepted interagency pass. No water at Artist Point; carry 2–3 liters per person and more during heat.
Food Pack breakfast and lunch. Wake ’N Bakery is the recovery stop if current Sunday hours work; North Fork Brewery or Chair 9 gives the route a dependable pizza dinner.

For the photographer

Shoot the light, not the checklist

The image set was sourced through Google Images from professional landscape portfolios and established travel publishers; weak, portrait, watermarked and near-duplicate candidates were removed.

Ridge light

Arrive before crowds

Use 24–70mm for layers and 70–200mm for compressed peaks. Do not step into meadow restoration areas for a foreground.

Water

Polarizer with restraint

Rotate while watching reflections; maximum polarization can kill the glacial color that makes the scene work.

Smoke

Make the decision early

Warm haze can look dramatic in one frame, but it is not a reason to remain outdoors in unhealthy air.
